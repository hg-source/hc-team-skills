# Evidence and deliberation

## Evidence ledger rules

Record each decision-relevant claim with a stable ID. Minimum fields:

- Claim and claim type: fact, inference, assumption, forecast, recommendation.
- Supporting or opposing hypothesis.
- Source title, URL or local path, publisher, publication date, and accessed date.
- Source class: primary, official, peer-reviewed, audited, reputable secondary, marketplace, anecdotal.
- Freshness, directness, independence family, limitations, and confidence.
- Owner and verification status.

Prefer primary or official evidence for regulation, specifications, trial results, financial disclosures, and product claims. Secondary sources can locate evidence or triangulate interpretation; they should not silently replace an accessible primary source.

## Claim-source fit

Reject these common substitutions:

- Configuration evidence presented as proof of live execution.
- Search-result titles presented as verified product facts.
- US demand presented as proof of Korean demand.
- Ingredient eligibility presented as finished-product approval.
- Laboratory plausibility presented as commercial shelf-life proof.
- Cost of goods presented as full contribution economics.
- Several pages repeating one press release presented as independent corroboration.

## Cross-examination protocol

For each leading hypothesis, answer:

1. What observation would make it false?
2. What is the strongest alternative explanation?
3. Which key claim rests on the weakest source?
4. Are the apparent corroborating sources independent?
5. What evidence is missing because it is hard to obtain?
6. Which assumption drives the recommendation most?
7. What would a regulator, operator, customer, finance owner, and skeptic object to?

## Lightweight ACH

Create a matrix of hypotheses by material evidence. Mark evidence as consistent, inconsistent, neutral, or diagnostic. Focus on evidence that discriminates between hypotheses. Count neither rows nor votes; explain diagnosticity and source quality.

## Assumption audit

Classify assumptions by impact and uncertainty:

- High impact / high uncertainty: validate or gate before commitment.
- High impact / low uncertainty: verify with authoritative evidence.
- Low impact / high uncertainty: bound with a range.
- Low impact / low uncertainty: record without expanding scope.

## Premortem and red team

Assume the recommendation failed after the relevant time horizon. Identify failure causes across customer, product, regulation, supply, economics, operations, brand, and execution. Convert credible causes into mitigations, leading indicators, kill gates, or experiments.

The minority report must preserve any dissent that could change the decision, even if the lead rejects it. State what evidence would make the minority view dominant.
